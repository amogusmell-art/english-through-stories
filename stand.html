Relevance

Modern youth spend massive amounts of free time inside English-language fantasy worlds (RPGs, films, long-running series) without realizing their educational value. A single role-playing game contains over 20,000 words. Fantasy builds rich invented vocabularies, repeating lexis, and emotional engagement, turning casual entertainment into natural, incidental language practice.

Goal

To investigate whether narrative, dialogue-rich fantasy media (games, films, and series) supports incidental English vocabulary acquisition through context and repetition more effectively than unsystematic media consumption.

Objectives
  • Review research on how video games, films, and series affect second-language acquisition.
  • Explain why the fantasy genre supports durable incidental vocabulary retention.
  • Build the practical web platform «English Through Stories» pairing fantasy titles with vocabulary.
  • Conduct two-stage testing (May baseline vs. post-summer) and a Google Forms learner survey.
  • Formulate practical recommendations for learners.
Methods
  • Theoretical: Literature review and comparative analysis (traditional classroom vs. immersive media learning).
  • Engineering: Single-file HTML web development hosted openly via GitHub.
  • Empirical: Two-stage pedagogical testing (May baseline & post-summer) and Google Forms learner surveying (n = 11).

Research Problem & Hypothesis

Problem: How can popular videogames, films, and series be transformed into structured, self-directed language acquisition tools?
Hypothesis: Narrative, dialogue-rich fantasy games and series teach English more effectively than passive media consumption because understanding the language is functionally necessary to follow the plot — not an optional extra.

MAIN RESULT
1. Facts & Empirical Data

Summer Experiment: Students completed a baseline English test in May, used the «English Through Stories» website and fantasy media during summer break, and took a post-test. Scores rose by +32.5% (from 55% to 87.5%).

Learner Survey (n = 11): 63.6% (7/11) learned English via movies, series, and games; 27.3% (3/11) credited school alone; 0% credited dedicated language apps (e.g., Duolingo).

2. Author's Analysis

Interactivity alone does not guarantee acquisition; comprehension must be required to advance the story. Narrative fantasy induces a psychological «state of flow» across four core skills: Vocabulary (contextual repetition), Listening (natural pace & reduced subtitle reliance), Speaking (multiplayer voice/text chat), and Culture (humour, idioms, and storytelling conventions).

3. Website Demonstration & Contribution

Developed the self-contained web platform «English Through Stories» categorizing Movies (Inception), Series (Sherlock, Stranger Things), and Games (Detroit: Become Human, Life is Strange, Portal 2) with level notes and dialogues.
Conclusions
  • Fantasy media does not replace classroom lessons, but serves as a powerful practical supplement.
  • Summer immersion using the project website raised test performance from 55% to 87.5% (+32.5%).
  • Incidental learning is strongest when understanding dialogue is essential to follow the plot.
  • Simple, open-source architecture ensures reliable learner access.
